    OpenHood
    Service Bay

    The visual rules for OpenHood, the app that lets auto repair customers inspect findings, ask their technician and approve repairs, and lets service advisors keep visits moving. Every element below shows a live sample and the CSS that draws it. Designers, developers, shop staff and course reviewers all work from this page.

    Blue shows the way

    Blue marks where you are and what you can tap: the current step, the active tab, links and icons.

    Teal moves the job forward

    Teal is the action color (Approve, Send) and the "done" color. One teal button per screen.

    Red and amber are only for status

    They appear on "Needs attention" and "Plan soon", always as a colored square plus a word.

    01

    Logo

    The mark is a blue tile with an up-right arrow: the car going up on the lift, and the customer moving forward. The wordmark is Overpass Bold in mixed case. The logo is an artwork, so its weight is fixed and separate from the type rules.

    OpenHoodPrimary · on navy
    OpenHoodOn white · navy wordmark
    App icon · 72 px
    Do
    • Keep clear space equal to half the mark's height on every side.
    • Minimum size: 24 px mark, 16 px wordmark.
    • Use the blue line under the top bar; it continues the mark's color.
    Don't
    • Place the blue tile on blue or on photos.
    • Set the wordmark in capitals or stretch it.
    • Recolor the mark in red, amber or teal.

    02

    Color

    Five brand colors from one palette. Navy, blue and teal do almost all the work. Red and amber are kept for status, so when they appear, people notice. Every text pair below passes WCAG AA.

    Brand · navy, blue, teal

    Status · red, amber (status only)

    Neutrals

    Do
    • Use plain blue #3F88C5 for fills, bars and icons; use --oh-blue-text for small blue text.
    • Put white text on teal or navy, never on plain blue (3.8 : 1).
    • Pair every status color with its tint and a word.
    Don't
    • Use red or amber for buttons, links, headings or decoration.
    • Put text on amber; it is only the status square.
    • Use --oh-line for input borders; use --oh-line-strong (3.3 : 1).

    05

    Buttons

    Buttons are Overpass Semibold in sentence case. The teal primary button has a darker bottom edge so it reads as something to press.

    Variants

    Sizes & states

    Do
    • One teal button per screen or dialog.
    • Use a verb and object, and add the price when money is involved: "Approve repair · $260".
    • Stack full-width buttons on phones, primary on top.
    Don't
    • Use red for "Decline"; declining is a normal choice, so use secondary.
    • Make a button plain blue; blue is for wayfinding, teal is for action.
    • Write vague labels like "OK" or "Submit".
    CSS · Buttons
    06

    Icons

    Line icons on a 24 px grid with a 1.8 px stroke and round caps, drawn in currentColor. Navy by default and blue when they lead a row or field. Sizes: 16 px next to text, 20 px in lists and tabs, 24 px on their own.

    16 20 24 .oh-icon--accent (blue)
    Do
    • Give icon-only buttons an aria-label.
    • Use the same icon for the same thing everywhere (clock = waiting time).
    Don't
    • Use emoji or filled icons, except play.
    • Use an icon alone for status; pair it with a word.

    Dialogs

    Dialogs confirm anything that authorizes work or money. They have a blue top edge, or red for destructive actions. On phones, use a bottom sheet so the buttons stay within thumb reach.

    Do
    • Write the title as a question that names the item: "Approve front brake repair?"
    • Restate the total and what happens next.
    • Trap focus, close on Esc, and return focus to the trigger.
    Don't
    • Use a dialog just to show information; use a notice instead.
    • Open a dialog on top of another dialog.

    08

    Menus

    Menus drop from a trigger on the advisor desk: filters, the account menu and actions on a work order. Icons are blue, hover is a pale blue tint, and destructive items are red below a rule.

    • Filter by advisor
    • RO-1048 · Priya Shah
    • Alex · Service advisor

    Try the "All advisors" trigger. Arrow keys move between items, and Esc closes the menu.

    Do
    • Group items under a mono label, and put destructive items below a rule.
    • End an item with "…" when it opens a dialog.
    Don't
    • Hide the only path to a main action inside a menu.
    • Put more than about 7 items in one menu.

    Forms

    Inputs are white with a 3.3 : 1 border. Focus adds a blue border and a soft blue ring. Checked controls turn teal. Labels sit above the field in Overpass Semibold.

    Chat composer

    Do
    • Always show a visible label; placeholders are only examples.
    • Write errors that say how to fix the problem.
    • Use mono for codes like VIN and RO numbers.
    Don't
    • Show an error before the person has finished the field.
    • Use a switch for a choice that needs a Save button; use a checkbox.
